# Datalink in travel

Notes on how travel reads uplink (1030 MHz) and datalink (VDL2, VHF ACARS,
HFDL, Airframes.io) messages, what the formats look like, and what we learned
along the way. Code: `Sources/Travel/Ingest.swift` (parsing),
`Dataset.swift` (merging, timeouts), `PlotPanel.swift` (display).

## How messages attach to aircraft

Everything is keyed by the ICAO 24-bit address, like jet1090. Dropping a
jet1090 recording together with its `_uplink.jsonl` and `_vdl2.jsonl` files
merges them per aircraft.

- An address seen only in uplink is not shown: most of the ~3,000 uplink
  addresses of a flight are parity noise.
- An address seen only in datalink is shown when a datalink message gives
  its position (see below); otherwise it waits for another source.

A line is routed by its keys: `uf` → uplink, `bearer` → datalink,
`mmsi` → ship162, `icao24` → jet1090.

## Uplink (1030 MHz)

Fields used: `timestamp`, `uf`, `icao24` (target), `interrogator`, `bds`,
`kind`, `mid`.

- UF 0 → ACAS, UF 16 → ACAS (`kind: "resolution"` → resolution advisory
  coordination), UF 24 → ELM, other formats → radar with its II/SI code.
- UF 16 broadcasts carry `icao24: "ffffff"` and the sender in `mid`; travel
  reads `mid`.
- Current uplink1030 output (`beta-output.md` there): `rssi` and
  `confidence` in `metadata`; UF16 `acas_b` puts the sender in `icao24`
  with `address_role: "sender"` and `ap: "ffffff"`; resolution messages
  keep the target in `icao24` and the sender in `mid`. `SI0` and
  `invalid` are not codes. Both layouts are read.
- Redis: uplink1030 `PUBLISH`es each event, as one JSONL line, on topic
  `uplink1030` (`--redis-topic`); new Redis sources subscribe to it.
- Shown as the *Interrogations* chart: one row per interrogator, one tick per
  interrogation, coloured by requested register; the time axis spans the
  uplink data only.

### Interrogators on the map

- `Resources/radars.csv` lists candidate sites per code, generated by
  `scripts/make-radars.py` from the uplink1030 repository:
  - curated French sites (`analysis/20261013/output/csv/french_ssr_sites.csv`,
    basis *located*), and approximate areas for codes without one
    (`radar_site_candidates.csv`, *approximate*);
  - `radar1090.txt` entries (code, country, name; commented lines included,
    as in the report) joined by country and first word of the name with
    the coordinates of `worldwide_radar_locations.geojson`, an unverified
    historical directory from 2014 (*historical*): 101 sites, Spain
    included (II5 Paracuellos, II9 Begas, II7 Monflorite, II3 Alcolea…).
    Failing that, the airport of the same name and country in
    `airports.json` (*airport*, the report's "airport area"): 72 sites,
    e.g. Bucharest, Constanta, Qabala, Aktau, Kostanay, Taraz. 215
    catalogue entries remain without coordinates; there are none at all
    for China, Korea or Japan.
  Assignments are undated candidates; the live fit below decides.
- Evidence is counted per aircraft and 10-minute segment, so that on a
  long flight the same aircraft can support different radars sharing a
  code (II8 is heard in Europe, the Caucasus and Kazakhstan).
- Recordings without `interrogator` (jet1090 before mid-2026): the code is
  recomputed from the DF 11 frame (parity minus CRC of the first 56 bits;
  some receivers store DF 11 frames 112 bits long). Checked against
  jet1090's own output (SI11, SI9, II0).
- jet1090 DF 11 `interrogator` is a string (`"SI12"`) now and was an object
  (`{"type": "SI", "value": 12}`) in October 2026 recordings; both are read.
- Sites are checked live against the data, as in the uplink1030 report
  (`Dataset.fitSites`), and refitted as data arrives (every 10 s live):
  - Evidence per code: aircraft it addresses (uplink roll-calls) and
    aircraft whose DF 11 all-call replies name it (jet1090 `interrogator`).
    An aircraft counts with ≥ 2 interrogations heard or ≥ 5 replies: single
    DF 11 replies are dominated by corrupted identifiers that still look
    legal (every SI code shows up otherwise).
  - Each aircraft, at the middle of its exchanges with the code, goes to
    the nearest candidate within 460 km; candidates with ≥ 3 aircraft and
    ≥ 10 % of the located ones are kept. Reused codes thus split into
    populations (SI12 Paris-Nord / Toulouse), each with its own period.
  - A fit is *consistent* with a median distance ≤ 350 km and ≤ 25 % of
    the located aircraft unexplained; otherwise *weak* (likely another
    radar using the code), drawn faded with `?` after the code, as the
    report writes plausible but unestablished names.
  - On 13 Nov: consistent SI26 Vaudringhem, II3 Palaiseau, SI30 Limours,
    II1 CDG area, SI12 Paris-Nord, II14 Limours; weak SI44 Gathemo, SI4,
    SI6, SI60, II14 Ymare. Codes heard but without candidate include SI28
    (Schiphol in radar1090.txt), SI10, SI19, SI24, SI45, SI58, II11.
  - On 30 Oct (LEMD→LFBO): consistent II5 Paracuellos (median 25 km,
    5.00 s), II9 Begas, II7 Monflorite, II2 Palma, SI60 Auch (4.995 s, as
    in the report), SI12 Toulouse (4.959 s, the report's southern
    population); II3 splits into Alcolea (5.03 s, weak) and Palaiseau.
  - `Travel --interrogators <files>` prints every code's fit to stdout.
- Rotation period: the report's estimator (per aircraft, burst onsets
  > 0.5 s apart, gaps 2–20 s, 0.025 s histogram, median within ±0.15 s
  of the mode, n ≥ 10), from interrogations heard on 1030 MHz only. On
  13 Nov: II3 3.967 s, SI26 5.891 s, SI30 4.021 s, SI28 4.188 s, SI54
  9.622 s; II1 2.017 s (4.00 s in October: a second source interleaving?).
- Badges show interrogators heard in the last 2 minutes, one per site.
  Hovering a badge, or an interrogator row in the interrogation chart,
  colours the aircraft it interrogated in the last two turns (at least
  15 s). Badges of interrogators addressing the selected aircraft blink at
  their rotation period, rounded to the second.
- **II0** is a valid identifier but a shared one (Annex 10: supplementary
  acquisition with lockout override, mostly PR=10 all-calls): several
  interrogators use it, so it gets no site and no period (see uplink1030
  `analysis/20261013/report.md`, "What exactly is II0?").
- DF 11 replies are timed by the radar beam: an aircraft answers an
  all-call when the main beam is on it (side lobes are suppressed), so a
  reply naming a code at time t gives the antenna azimuth then, i.e. the
  bearing from the radar to that aircraft = θ0 ± 360°·t/P. The 1090
  receiver hears replies from all around, unlike the 1030 one.
  Checked on 13 Nov against reviewed sites: phase coherence of
  bearing − rotation over 2-minute windows 0.95 for SI26 from Vaudringhem
  (P = 5.88470 s, 0.90 over the whole flight), 0.89 for SI30 from Limours,
  0.44 for II3 from Palaiseau (random ≈ 0.1). This validates a candidate.
  Searching the position with the same score is ambiguous from one moving
  receiver (as good 128 km away for SI26): the aircraft heard in a window
  are bunched compared with their distance to the radar. A ground
  receiver hearing aircraft all around a radar should do better; not
  implemented yet.
- From 1030 MHz alone, an on-board receiver cannot locate radars: it only
  hears a radar when the beam points at its own aircraft, so interrogation
  bearings are all alike. Rotation periods, however, are
  measured precisely (e.g. SI26 5.885 s, II3 3.967 s).

## Datalink layouts

datalink has written three layouts; travel reads all of them.

| Layout | When | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Old envelope | June 2026 (`analysis/datalink_june15.jsonl`) | `{"event", "aircraft": {icao24}, "receiver": {channel_hz}, "message": {"kind": "avlc", "data": {…, "lcf": "S", "payload": {"kind": "acars", "data": {…}}}}}` |
| Keyed envelope | Nov 2026 recordings (`~/20261112_*_vdl2.jsonl`) | `{"event", "aircraft", "receiver", "message": {"avlc": {…, "lcf": {"S": {"sfunc": …}}, "payload": {"acars": {…}}}}}` |
| Flat (beta) | current build (`~/20260615_AA83895248_LFBO_EHAM_vdl2.jsonl`) | `{"timestamp", "frame", "bearer", "icao24", "registration", "flight_id", "direction", "station", "station_name", "avlc": {"lcf": "S", "sfunc": …}, "acars": {…}, "x25": {…}, "arinc622": {…}, "kinematics": {…}, "metadata": [{channel_hz, signal_dbfs, noise_dbfs, freq_offset_hz, …}]}` |

Airframes.io records, as published on Redis, look like:

```
{"bearer", "icao24", "registration", "aircraft_id", "timestamp",
 "airframes": {"payload": {label, text, frequency, tail, from_hex, to_hex,
                           link_direction, airframe: {icao, tail}, flight: {…}, …},
               "src": {addr_type, icao24}, "dst": {…},
               "app": {"text"|"arinc622"|"squitter"|"atis_request"|…: {…}}},
 "kinematics": {…}, "metadata": [{"format": "airframes.io", …}]}
```

Display:

- Summary from the innermost layer: `ACARS <label>`, `X.25 <packet type>`,
  `XID`, or the AVLC function (`Receive ready`, `Selective reject`…).
- ARINC 622 messages are named from `imi`: ADS → ADS-C, DIS → ADS-C
  disconnect, AT1 → CPDLC, CR1/CC1/DR1 → CPDLC logon/connect/disconnect,
  AB1 → AOC; the `atsu_address` (e.g. `RUHAAYA`) is shown as the station.
- Other Airframes applications are appended (`· Atis Request`).
- The bearer prefixes the summary unless it is `vdl2`, `vhf`/ACARS,
  `decoded` or `unknown` (e.g. `HFDL ACARS H1`).
- With `station_name`, the airport code (first word) replaces the station
  address; the full name is in the tooltip.
- The same frame is often decoded on 2–4 neighbouring 25 kHz channels at
  once; travel merges identical messages within 1 s and shows `136.850 +2`.

## Positions from datalink

`kinematics.derived_from` says where a position comes from (see
`crates/acars/src/decode/compact.rs` in datalink):

| `derived_from` | Source | Used |
|---|---|---|
| `adsc_basic` | ADS-C basic report (position, altitude) | yes |
| `adsc_earth`, `adsc_air` | ADS-C track / heading only | track |
| `cpdlc`, `cpdlc_position_report` | CPDLC | yes |
| `label32`, AOC formats | ACARS position messages | yes |
| `vdl2_xid_aircraft_location` | VDL2 XID | yes |
| `hfdl_performance`, `hfdl_frequency` | HFDL | yes |
| `airframes_flight` | Airframes.io flight tracking, ~0.1° | position and track, not altitude |
| `squitter` | **ground station** position | no |
| `oooi_qf`, `oooi_qq` | no position | — |

Airframes flight-tracking altitudes can be ten times too large (255,000 ft
seen), hence ignored.

Datalink positions are kept apart from received ADS-B positions
(`Series.reported`) and only used for aircraft without positions of their
own, so that coarse points do not make jet1090 tracks zigzag.

### ADS-C

The report lives in `arinc622.payload.adsc` (flat layout) or
`airframes.app.arinc622.payload.adsc` (Airframes on Redis), as a list of
single-key tags (`AdscTag` in `crates/acars/src/decode/payload/arinc622/adsc.rs`):

- `basic_report` and the same layout in `emergency_basic_report`,
  `lateral_deviation_change_event`, `vertical_rate_change_event`,
  `altitude_range_event`, `waypoint_change_event`: `latitude`, `longitude`,
  `altitude_ft`, `timestamp_seconds_past_hour`.
- `earth_reference_data`: `true_track_degrees`, `ground_speed_kt`,
  `vertical_speed_ft_per_min`.
- `air_reference_data`: `true_heading_degrees`, `mach`.
- `meteo_data`: wind and temperature (not used yet).
- `flight_id`: `callsign`.
- `predicted_route`, `intermediate_projection`, `fixed_projection`: not used yet.

travel reads these tags directly, because `kinematics` is not enough:

- It only keeps the basic report (no track or speed).
- On Airframes records datalink merges `raw.merge(app)`
  (`crates/datalink/src/airframes.rs`), so the Airframes flight position
  wins over the ADS-C report and is labelled `airframes_flight`.

When an ADS-C position is present, `kinematics` positions are ignored. The
report time is rebuilt from the reception time and
`timestamp_seconds_past_hour`.

In Airframes captures, ADS-C contract requests are label A6 (uplink) and
reports label B6 (downlink). Messages from Airframes may carry a prefix
before the envelope (`L91ASQ0421/SKYSWSQ.ADS.…`); datalink strips it.

### Timeouts

ADS-B aircraft disappear 60 s after their last message. Aircraft located
only by datalink stay 30 minutes (`Dataset.reportTimeout`), with their
altitude and speed, and move along the line between two reports less than
2 h apart.

## Ground stations

Ground stations are drawn on the map (grey antenna badge with the airport
code, details in the tooltip) from the first time they are heard, at their
latest reported place.

- **ACARS squitters** (label SQ, Redis topic `datalink-sq`): broadcast by
  the ground station, no aircraft address. datalink decodes them as
  `app.squitter` (`SquitterMessage` in
  `crates/acars/src/decode/payload/arinc620/squitter.rs`):
  `{"version": 2, "kind": "X", "station": "AMSN", "airport": "KMSN",
  "latitude": 43.133, "longitude": -89.333, "link": "VHF",
  "frequency_mhz": 136.975, "provider": "ARINC", "status": 1, "raw": …}`.
  Version 0 and 1 squitters carry no position and are skipped. On Airframes
  records they sit in `airframes.app.squitter`; travel also reads a
  top-level `squitter`. Stations are keyed by provider and station code.
- **VDL2 station fields** in the flat layout: `station_name` (`EHAM`),
  `station_full_name` (`Amsterdam Schiphol`), `station_latitude`,
  `station_longitude` (since the 13 Nov 2026 redecode). Earlier output put
  the coordinates at the end of `station_name`
  (`LFBO Toulouse Blagnac France 43°36'N 001°24'E`); both are read.
  Stations are keyed by their address. Some stations come without
  coordinates (LFOB 215828, LFBT 2610a5 on 13 Nov): when `station_name`
  is an ICAO code, the airport position from jet1090's `airports.json`
  (rs1090 data, bundled) stands in for it.
- Datalink envelopes of the selected aircraft get a thin dashed line to
  their station (orange downlink, cyan uplink) when the message gives the
  station's position; otherwise only the envelope.
- Not used yet: VDL2 XID ground station frames (`xid.vdl_params.gs_location`,
  `{lat, lon}` in tenths of degrees): no example recorded so far.

In the Airframes 1-hour capture (`analysis/airframes_live_20260522T083132Z_1h.jsonl`),
the 400 SQ messages decode into 325 positions from 63 stations, mostly in
North America (395 over VHF ACARS, 5 over VDL2). Squitter `kinematics`
(`derived_from: squitter`) are the station's position, never the aircraft's.

Station badges give way to the markers of the selected aircraft where they
overlap.

## Redis

datalink publishes one topic per application (`redis_topic_for_record` in
`crates/datalink/src/util.rs`): `datalink-acars`, `datalink-x25`,
`datalink-cpdlc`, `datalink-adsc`, `datalink-sq`, `datalink-xid`,
`datalink-vdl2`, `datalink-hfdl`, `datalink-unknown`.

travel subscribes to channel names containing `*`, `?` or `[` with
`PSUBSCRIBE`; new Redis sources default to `jet1090 ship162 datalink-*`.
Sources saved before keep their channels.

`PUBSUB CHANNELS` only lists channels with subscribers, so listening is the
way to see what is published (`uvx iredis`, or a raw socket).

## Open points in datalink

- The `Bearer` enum maps `acars`/`vhf`, `vdl`/`vdl2`, `hf`/`hfdl`;
  satellite ACARS (`aero-acars`), which carries most oceanic ADS-C, becomes
  `unknown`.
- `kinematics` on Airframes records prefers Airframes tracking over the
  decoded ADS-C/CPDLC position; the reverse would be more precise.
- One LFPG–EHAM message was attributed to an EGLL station: worth checking
  in `VDL2_Ground_Stations.txt`.
- The ADS-C layout on Redis was checked against the Rust types and the
  `decode arinc622` output, not yet against a live `datalink-adsc` message.

## Layout suggestions (13 Nov 2026)

Compared with jet1090 (`timestamp`, `frame`, `df`, `icao24`, decoded fields
at the top, `metadata: [{rssi, serial, name}]`) and uplink (`timestamp`,
`frame`, `uf`, `icao24`, `rssi`, `confidence`), we suggested for datalink:

1. Same top level as jet1090: `timestamp`, `frame`, `icao24` (always the
   aircraft), plus `registration`, `flight_id`. *Done in the flat layout.*
2. Explicit `station`, `station_name`, `direction`. *Done.*
3. Internally tagged variants (`{"lcf": "S", "sfunc": …}`) rather than the
   variant name as key. *Done.*
4. One object per layer side by side (`avlc`, `x25`, `acars`, application).
   *Done.*
5. Merge copies from neighbouring channels into one line with several
   `metadata` entries. *Not done (no deduplication, by choice).*
6. A schema version. *Not done, by choice.*

For uplink: move `rssi` and `confidence` into `metadata`, and put the ACAS
sender in `icao24` instead of `ffffff` + `mid` (planned in datalink's
`analysis/BETA_FLAT_OUTPUT_PLAN.md`).
